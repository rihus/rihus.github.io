The hyperpolarized signal is precious: it fades with every radio-frequency pulse and
every second that passes, and the whole scan has to fit into a single breath-hold. I work
on the **physics of how the data are collected and reconstructed** to make the most of
that limited signal.

This includes using **spiral acquisition** patterns that cover more data per pulse,
correcting the image for uneven radio-frequency transmission across the lungs
(**flip-angle and B₁ corrections**), and reconstruction methods that account for how the
signal decays during the scan (for example keyhole and compressed-sensing approaches
developed with colleagues). Together, these steps give images that are quicker to acquire
and in which brightness more truly reflects how much gas reached each part of the lung —
which matters when the images are used to make measurements.
