Standard MRI is very good at imaging tissue that contains water, but the lungs are mostly
air, so they appear almost black. In **hyperpolarized ¹²⁹Xe MRI**, a person breathes in a
small amount of xenon gas whose magnetic signal has been boosted many thousands of times
in a special device called a polarizer. During a short breath-hold, the scanner can then
"see" the gas itself as it fills the lungs.

The resulting images show, region by region, where the inhaled gas reaches and where it
does not. Areas that do not fill with gas — called *ventilation defects* — can reveal
problems that a standard whole-lung breathing test may miss, without any ionizing
radiation. That makes the method well suited to repeated scans in children and
to tracking how a patient responds to treatment over time.
