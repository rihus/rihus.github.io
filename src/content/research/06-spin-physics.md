MRI is built on nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR): atomic nuclei behave like tiny magnets
that can be nudged with radio waves and "listened to". During my PhD at the University of
Parma I used NMR and nuclear quadrupole resonance (NQR), together with muon spin
spectroscopy and neutron scattering, to study materials at the atomic scale.

This work included showing coherent control of nuclear spins in a lanthanide molecule
coupled to an electron spin — a candidate building block for quantum information
processing — and using ⁵⁵Mn NMR to study magnetocaloric alloys of interest for magnetic
refrigeration. This physics foundation underpins my current work on MRI pulse sequences
and signal modelling.
